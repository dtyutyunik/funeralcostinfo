# Competitor Comparison — FuneralCostInfo vs. Published National Funeral Costs

Research date: September 23, 2026.
Purpose: benchmark FuneralCostInfo's numbers against what the main competitors publish today, and identify methodology gaps we can exploit.

## Our numbers (baseline for comparison)

NFDA 2023 General Price List Study national medians (2023 dollars) — our anchors:
| Service type | Our anchor |
|---|---|
| Funeral with viewing + burial | $8,300 |
| Funeral with viewing + burial + vault | $9,995 |
| Funeral with viewing + cremation | $6,280 |
| Direct cremation | $2,750 |
| Immediate/direct burial | $3,720 |

State estimates = anchor × (state BEA 2024 RPP ÷ 100), ±15% illustrative range.
Example state points (traditional burial, 2023 dollars): CA $9,190 · TX $8,060 · FL $8,580 · NY $8,960 · MS $7,220.

## Takeaways

1. **Our anchors are the reference dataset, not an outlier.** NFDA's 2023 GPL medians ($8,300 / $9,995 / $6,280 / $2,750 / $3,720) are what everyone else quotes — Choice Mutual, Mutual of Omaha, Funeralwise, and Dignity-adjacent insurance content all start from the same numbers. Differences vs competitors are mostly artifacts of (a) **average vs median** (Funeralocity averages run ~5–13% above our median-based state figures) and (b) **vintage** (sites still quoting the 2021 study's $7,848/$6,970 make us look ~6% high, but we're the current ones).
2. **Our planned v3 (2026-dollar) numbers will land right on top of Funeralocity's observed averages.** $8,300 × ~1.1046 ≈ $9,170 vs Funeralocity's ~$8,600 national / $8,792 TX — convergence with the strongest competitor methodology validates the inflation-adjustment approach. Parting Stone's blog already published the same adjustment ($9,170 / $11,040 / $6,940), so we should cite our own BLS derivation to differentiate.
3. **Direct cremation is where we're highest relative to the market.** Our $2,750 anchor sits above Funeralocity state averages (~$1,939–$2,295), Parting city listings (~$1,000–$2,400), Lincoln Heritage ($1,111–$1,735), Dignity ($2,205), and Empathy ($800–$1,200). The NFDA median reflects full-service funeral homes with a supplied container; the market clearly has a long low tail. We should explain this in methodology copy — or the figure reads as expensive.
4. **Nobody else does state-level modeling from official statistics.** Funeralocity has real GPL aggregation (best methodology, but averages-only and no 2026 adjustment); Lincoln Heritage has state pages with opaque, oddly low figures; everyone else is national-only. Our BEA-RPP-based state layer + 2026-dollar adjustment is a genuine differentiator — provided we document it.
5. **Cemetery costs are the industry's blind spot and our biggest content edge.** NFDA excludes them; Dignity admits totals "easily exceed $10,000" with cemetery but publishes no components; competitors hand-wave ($12k–$17.5k ranges with no sourcing). Our FCA-survey-based add-on model (plot, opening/closing, marker) is more specific than anything found in this research.

## Methodology gaps competitors have that we can exploit
- **Staleness:** multiple sites (Titan Casket, Policygenius, Empathy's report, Black Enterprise) present 2021 NFDA figures as current. A visible "data vintage" label on every figure beats them.
- **Median-vs-average confusion:** Choice Mutual, Legacy.com, and others mislabel NFDA medians as averages. A one-paragraph explainer on our methodology page turns this into trust.
- **No reproducible adjustment:** Parting Stone did the 2023→2026 inflation step but didn't show its work. We can publish the exact BLS series and factor.
- **Opaque "averages":** Lincoln Heritage and Dignity publish averages with no stated sample or method. We can contrast with fully sourced anchors (NFDA + BLS + BEA).
- **Missing state variation:** no competitor except Funeralocity (price collection) and Lincoln Heritage (unverifiable) gives state-level numbers. Our RPP model covers all 50 states from one documented formula.
