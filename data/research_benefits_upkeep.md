## 1. VA burial benefits (verify annually — amounts reset every Oct 1, start of federal fiscal year)

Source: https://www.va.gov/burials-memorials/veterans-burial-allowance/ (amounts table)
Source: https://www.va.gov/resources/what-does-burial-in-a-va-national-cemetery-include/ (national cemetery inclusions)

### Allowance amounts (deaths on/after Oct 1, 2025 — current as of Sep 2026)
- **Service-connected death** (died on/after Sept 11, 2001): **up to $2,000** burial allowance. (Died before Sept 11, 2001: $1,500.)
- **Non-service-connected death**: **$1,002 burial allowance + $1,002 plot/interment allowance**.
- **Headstone/marker allowance** (private cemetery): **$441**.
- **Burial in a VA national cemetery** — at no cost to the family: gravesite, opening and closing of the grave, government burial liner, headstone or marker, perpetual (ongoing) care of the gravesite, burial flag, Presidential Memorial Certificate, military funeral honors. VA may also reimburse some or all transportation costs to a national cemetery.

### Eligibility (one line each)
- Veteran must have been discharged under conditions other than dishonorable.
- Plus ONE of: died of a service-connected disability; died while receiving VA care (or at a VA-contracted facility); died while on authorized VA travel; died while receiving (or entitled to) VA pension/compensation; or had a VA compensation/pension claim pending at death.
- Claimant must have paid the costs and not be reimbursed elsewhere (spouse, child, parent, executor, or even a friend/funeral home can claim).

### Disclaimers needed
- Benefits are **not automatic — must be claimed** (VA Form 21P-530EZ). Non-service-connected claims must be filed **within 2 years of burial**.
- Surviving spouses listed on the veteran's profile get the plot/interment/transportation payment automatically on notice of death.
- Amounts change every Oct 1 — this page needs an annual refresh check.
- **Scam warning worth surfacing**: AARP (2026 guide) warns that "$25,000 VA burial benefit" messages are fraudulent; real 2026 allowances are up to $1,002 (non-service-connected) / $2,000 (service-connected). Source: https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/home-and-family/voices/veterans/2026/Veterans_Burial_Benefits_Guide_2026.pdf

## 2. Upkeep / perpetual care

### (a) State perpetual/endowment care mandates
The pattern: most states require cemeteries to deposit a slice of each plot sale into a trust whose **principal is held forever** and only the income funds maintenance. Key: it's usually **baked into the plot price**, not a separate add-on.

- **California** — Health & Safety Code §8738 (AB 651, operative Jan 1, 2024): **10% of net price or $250 (grave) / $150 (niche) / $250 (crypt), whichever is greater**, capped at $10,000 per space. Source: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB651/id/2435652
- **Texas** — Health & Safety Code Ch. 712: for sales since 9/1/2003, **$1.75 per sq ft OR 15% of purchase price, whichever is greater**. Source: TX Dept. of Banking perpetual-care annual statement form, https://www.dob.texas.gov/sites/default/files/files/Applications-Forms-Publications/Applications-Forms/pccas.pdf
- **Florida** — Ch. 497, F.S. requires care-and-maintenance trust deposits, commonly cited as **10% of price** — exact subsection unverified (found cited as "10% or $25, whichever is greater" in an abandoned-space-resale context, s. 497.268). **Needs verification before publishing.**
- **Delaware** — 1/10 of gross sales price (12 Del.C. §3554). Smaller state; clean statutory example. Source: https://www.casemine.com/act/us/59197568add7b05bd4dbd03a
- General: SEC no-action letter (1998) notes most states require perpetual-care trusts funded by a percentage of plot-sale revenues, principal maintained in perpetuity. Source: https://www.SEC.Gov/divisions/investment/noaction/1998/servicecorpint100698.pdf

### What's covered vs NOT covered
- **Covered**: common grounds — mowing, trimming, walkways, landscaping, general cemetery repair/upkeep (TX bill analysis: "mowing, trimming, or maintaining walkways and common grounds"). Source: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/85R/analysis/pdf/HB01948E.pdf
- **NOT covered**: individual markers/headstones — these are the family's responsibility. Evidence: cemeteries charge separately for straightening, resetting, and washing markers (see price list below), and VA explicitly lists "perpetual care of the gravesite" as a *benefit* precisely because private cemeteries' care funds don't cover everything.

### (b) Headstone/monument maintenance — real price points
Mount Lebanon Cemetery (NY), price list **effective Jan 1, 2025**, charges approved by the NY State Division of Cemeteries. Source: https://www.mountlebanoncemetery.com/static/MLC-PriceList-2025-Misc-Charges.pdf
- Wash monument: footstone **$88**, single **$114**, double **$140**, family **$182–$234**
- Straighten monument: single **$171**, double **$187**, family **$203–$218**
- Raise & reset (reset & grout): single **$260**, double **$291**, family **$322–$353**
- Repoint monument: single **$78**, double **$94**, family **$109**

Suggested calculator framing: one-time "marker upkeep over the years" range of **~$90–$350 per service event** (cleaning at the low end, reset at the high end), with a disclaimer that frequency is every several years at most and many families never pay it.

## 3. Cash advance items (standard NFDA category — third-party goods/services the funeral home passes through)

Present as **ranges with "varies by market" disclaimers** — these are industry-published ranges, not designed surveys.

### Newspaper obituary
- 2025 average US newspaper obituary: **$200–$500** overall; longer tributes in urban areas routinely **$800+**; photo add **$25–$250**. Source: https://obitsonline.net (note: URL absent from live-check results; text from search snippet)
- Local newspapers: **$100–$400**; major metro papers: **$300–$1,000+**; online memorials: free–$150. Source: https://www.elayne.com/resources/how-to-place-an-obituary-publishing-submitting-and-costs-explained (June 2026)
- NYT: $477–$14,550, avg $4,071 (outlier, major national paper); Legacy.com online obit: $150. Source: https://www.tributes.com/writing/obituary-cost (updated 5/27/2025)
- Newspaper ~$200 average for a 4–5 sentence obit with B&W photo. Source: https://everloved.com/articles/obituaries-and-funeral-announcements/how-to-create-an-online-obituary/

### Funeral flowers
- Small sympathy arrangement: **$65–$100**; standing spray: **$150–$350**; casket spray: **$250–$600+**; wreaths/memorial pieces: **$150–$500+**. Source: https://flowershoplakecharles.com/blog/how-much-should-funeral-flowers-cost-in-lake-charles-louisiana.html
- Florist casket spray **$250–$600+** (DIY $60–$120). Source: https://titancasket.com/blogs/funeral-guides-and-more/how-to-make-a-casket-spray-simple-step-by-step-guide
- Budget standing sprays/wreaths from **$155–$225** (discount florist, La Mirada CA). Source: https://www.funeralflowersforless.com/sympathy-flowers/standing-sprays

### Clergy / celebrant honorarium
- Own congregation clergy: **$150–$300**; outside clergy: **$250–$500**; non-affiliated officiant/celebrant: **$300–$800**. Source: https://thevaledictory.com/funeral-officiant-honorarium-payment/
- Commonly cited: clergy **$100–$300**, professional celebrants **$200–$500**. Source: https://skycaskets.com/blog/working-with-funeral-officiant-and-speakers/
- Senior-citizen cost roundup: clergy honorarium **$200–$300**. Source: https://www.seniorresource.com/how-much-does-an-average-funeral-cost/
- Note: church facility fees can stack on top (one 2025 church fee sheet: sanctuary usage $250, clergy honorarium $350 customary, organist $200). Source: https://www.fumc-cs.org/_files/ugd/ed4bd3_237808c2183042ccaeb62bd611bcc725.pdf

### Death certificates
- **$5–$35 per copy depending on the state**; families typically need several copies. Source: https://www.seniorresource.com/how-much-does-an-average-funeral-cost/
- **State-by-state fee table unsourced** — present the range with "varies by state" disclaimer.

### Bundled reference
One consumer roundup gives the whole cash-advance cluster as: flowers **$200–$400**, obituary **$100–$800+**, clergy **$200–$300**, death certificates **$5–$35/copy**. Source: https://www.seniorresource.com/how-much-does-an-average-funeral-cost/

## 4. Body donation to science

### Science Care (for-profit national program)
- Donation is **FREE** through Science Care — includes **free cremation, transport of the body, and filing of the death certificate**, "saving potentially thousands of dollars in funeral costs."
- Cremated remains typically returned to the family within **3–5 weeks**.
- **No age limit**; pre-registration not required; organ donors can also be whole-body donors.
- Source: https://www.sciencecare.com/blog/how-to-donate-your-body-to-science and https://www.sciencecare.com/blog/what-if-i-want-to-donate-but-family-wants-funeral

### MedCure (nonprofit program, accredited by the American Association of Tissue Banks)
- **No-cost** end-of-life option. Once accepted at time of final transportation, covers: **transportation arrangements, one certified copy of the death certificate, cremation, and either return of cremated remains to the family or scattering at sea**.
- **No upper age restrictions**; accepts wide variety of medical conditions.
- **Exclusions** (most common reasons for ineligibility): HIV/AIDS or hepatitis B/C, history of IV drug use, recent prolonged incarceration/institutionalization/homelessness, severely over- or underweight at time of passing.
- Sources: https://medcure.org/veterans-benefits-and-body-donation/ and brochure https://medcure.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/MedCure_Brochure_R1.pdf

### The catches (disclaimers needed)
- **Open-casket funerals are not possible** with whole-body donation; memorial services happen after ashes are returned (MedCure brochure).
- **Final acceptance is determined at time of death** — pre-registration does not guarantee acceptance (screening questions at time of passing).
- Body must be transferred to the program **promptly**; families are told not to engage a funeral home themselves at time of passing (MedCure FAQ).
- **University willed-body programs** follow the same general model but timelines vary (some keep remains a semester to ~2 years before cremation; return-of-ashes policies differ by school) — **program-specific details unsourced; present only the general model with a "check the specific program" disclaimer.**
